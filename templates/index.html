What are Skin Diseases?

Skin diseases are conditions that affect your skin. These diseases may cause rashes, inflammation, itchiness or other skin changes. Some skin conditions may be genetic, while lifestyle factors may cause others. Skin disease treatment may include medications, creams or ointments, or lifestyle changes.

Skin Diseases

Enfeksiyonel

"Enfeksiyonel" refers to anything related to an infection caused by microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites. These infections can affect various parts of the body, leading to symptoms ranging from mild discomfort to severe illness. Infections can spread through direct contact, airborne particles, contaminated food or water, and insect bites, making prevention and proper hygiene essential.

Ekzama

Eczema is an inflammatory skin condition characterized by itching, redness, dryness, and sometimes fluid-filled blisters. It often develops as a result of stress, allergens, chemicals, or genetic predisposition. It most commonly appears on the face, hands, inner elbows, and behind the knees. Treatment involves moisturizers, corticosteroid creams, and avoiding triggers.

Akne

Acne is a skin condition that occurs when the oil glands in the skin become clogged, leading to blackheads, whiteheads, and inflamed pimples. It is commonly seen during puberty due to hormonal changes. Acne frequently appears on areas with a high concentration of oil glands, such as the face, back, and chest. It can be managed through cleanliness, suitable skincare products, and, if necessary, dermatological treatments.

Pigment

Pigment is the natural substance in the body that gives color to the skin, hair, and eyes. An increase or decrease in melanin production can cause dark or light spots on the skin. Factors like sun exposure, hormonal changes, or certain health conditions can lead to pigment disorders. These issues can be managed through creams, laser treatments, or dermatological care.

Benign

"Benign" refers to a condition, tumor, or growth that is not harmful or malignant. It is typically used to describe non-cancerous conditions that do not spread to other parts of the body. Benign tumors or lesions are usually slower-growing, and while they may cause discomfort or other symptoms, they generally do not pose a serious health risk. In many cases, benign conditions can be monitored or treated without the need for aggressive medical intervention.

Malign

"Malign" refers to a condition, tumor, or growth that is harmful, cancerous, and has the potential to spread to other parts of the body. Malignant tumors are typically aggressive, growing rapidly and invading surrounding tissues. They can spread through the bloodstream or lymphatic system to distant organs, a process known as metastasis. Malignant conditions often require more intensive treatment, such as surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation therapy, and pose a significant health risk if not addressed promptly.
